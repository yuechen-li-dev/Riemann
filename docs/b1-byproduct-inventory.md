# B1 · Five mathematical byproducts

B1 produced a replayable inventory at corpus revision `a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6`. The five results concern five different tasks: resolving singularities, describing prime distributions, decomposing characters, comparing valuation stabilizers, and certifying optimal allocations. Chebotarev stands out for reuse in number theory. Cyclic duality is the best entry point for independent checking. Its two-letter specialization admits exact rational certificates, and its substantive Lean proof is in one module besides the statement and Mathlib.

All five have recognizable mathematical antecedents. The evidence supports no claim of new mathematics or priority of formalization. In particular, the separate Hironaka project has a closely related formal theorem. Chebotarev has a useful stronger endpoint at the statement level than the single-class statement in the external project checked here. That comparison is about scope, and says little about which project produced a verified proof first.

Go authenticates the archive, extracts the original headers, checks cited definitions, scoped context, imports and selected proof uses, rejects unsupported evidence labels, and verifies 405 exact finite primal/dual certificates. Every record remains **structurally reviewed**. The source contains theorems with ordinary mathematical hypotheses and no RH or GRH prerequisite in the reviewed statements. ## 1. Functorial resolution

**Declaration.** `FunctorialResolution.functorialResolutionCharZero` supplies one rule \(R\) for every characteristic-zero field \(k\) and reduced scheme \(X\to\operatorname{Spec}k\) whose structure morphism is quasi-compact and locally of finite type. The rule assigns a finite initial segment of a tower of blowups, identified with \(X\) at zero. Blowup is specified by making the pullback ideal effective Cartier and by a universal factorization property. The top is smooth over \(k\) and proper over \(X\), restricts to an isomorphism over the smooth locus, and has dense preimage of that locus. Centers lie over the nonsmooth locus. The reduced inverse image of that locus is strict normal crossings. At each point its ideal on an affine open is generated by a product of a subset of functions whose germs form a regular system of parameters in the regular local ring. The empty product covers empty support. These are assertions about the nonsmooth boundary of \(X/k\), with no input boundary required. [Claim and immediate supplier](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Development/ResolutionOfSingularities/HasSimpleNormalCrossingsOfInStrictNormalCrossing.lean#L15-L18) · [Scheme and tower definitions](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Development/ResolutionOfSingularities/Statements.lean#L9-L153)

**Compatibility.** For each over-\(k\) smooth morphism with a specified relative dimension, and for each field extension with reduced base change as stated, the source asks for isomorphisms of the new tower to the corresponding pullback stages. The index map strictly increases over the retained steps and ends at the original terminal index. The condition identifies retained steps with the centers whose pullback has nonempty support, and requires the isomorphisms to respect structural maps, steps, and center ideals. The existential \(R\) precedes these universal requirements. The proof wrapper uses `EmbeddedResolution.strongResolutionOfSingularitiesCharZero`. [Comparison predicates](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Development/ResolutionOfSingularities/Statements.lean#L91-L153)

**Comparison.** This is in the tradition of Hironaka 1964 and Kollár’s functorial formulation. At external revision `044d89c9455859446fc5e986667319f910e15f4b`, `AlgebraicGeometry.exists_functorial_resolution` supplies a `BlowUpSequenceFunctor` on *reduced equidimensional* schemes with strong resolution, smooth compatibility and field-extension compatibility. The corpus input has no equidimensionality restriction. The external result packages functorial structure differently, and the precise implications between its strong-resolution and compatibility predicates and the corpus’s explicit stage and center equalities remain **not yet determined**. Formalization novelty records the known external formalization of the result family. This is a substantial assertion about singular schemes, including an actual terminal smooth object. [Hironaka, Annals 1964](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mat388) · [Kollár, Theorem 36](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400837086) · [External Lean statement](https://github.com/resolutionorg/hironaka/blob/044d89c9455859446fc5e986667319f910e15f4b/Hironaka/Resolution/Algebraic/Kol07/Thm36/Theorem36.lean)

## 2. Chebotarev density for conjugacy-stable sets

**Declaration.** `GroundTheory.Global.hasDirichletDensity_chebotarevSet` uses section variables \(K,L\) that are number fields, with an algebra structure and \(L/K\) Galois. It quantifies over \(C\subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\), assuming \(\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}\in C\) for each \(\sigma\in C\) and each \(\tau\). Its prime set consists of height-one primes \(v\) in \(\mathcal O_K\) for which every \(w\) over \(v\) has ramification index one and arithmetic Frobenius in \(C\). The equivalent formulation with a Frobenius witness over \(v\) is also given in the file. For that set \(S\), the ratio
\[
\frac{\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S}(\operatorname{absNorm}\mathfrak p)^{-s}}
     {\sum_{\mathfrak p}(\operatorname{absNorm}\mathfrak p)^{-s}}
\]
converges to \(|C|/|\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)|\) as \(s\to1^+\). A nonempty class has positive density, so the claim gives infinitely many primes of the specified splitting type. The proof peels off one conjugacy class with the single-class theorem and adds disjoint Dirichlet densities. [Corpus statement](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/GroundTheory/GlobalFields/TateThesis/Chebotarev.lean#L198-L237) · [Prime set](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/GroundTheory/GlobalFields/TateThesis/ChebotarevCount.lean#L14-L19) · [Mathlib density at the submitted pin](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/DirichletDensity.lean)

**Comparison.** This is the usual Chebotarev theorem, extended to finite disjoint unions of classes. At revision `c64095e6cc6483b401849c7fd9182d983d3bf261`, CBirkbeck's Lean project states a single-class endpoint, and the displayed declaration ends in `sorry`. Its prime set is expressed using ideals, and its density definition likewise normalizes by a prime-ideal series. The corpus has a genuinely broader input and proof text for the union step. A bridge would identify the ideal and height-one spectrum definitions and supply finite additivity. Priority, and completion of the corpus’s analytic proof closure, remain unresolved. A targeted Mathlib tree search found the density API. It provides no evidence about formalizations whose files use different names. [Class field theory course](https://kokunoyumeto.github.io/open-math-courses-public/courses/NT-CFT/the-chebotarev-density.html) · [External declaration](https://github.com/CBirkbeck/chebotarev-density/blob/c64095e6cc6483b401849c7fd9182d983d3bf261/CebotarevDensity/Main.lean) · [External density definition](https://github.com/CBirkbeck/chebotarev-density/blob/c64095e6cc6483b401849c7fd9182d983d3bf261/CebotarevDensity/Density.lean)

## 3. Brauer induction for automorphism groups

**Declaration.** `GroundTheory.LocalField.IsCharacter.mem_spanZ_isElementary` quantifies over fields \(K,L\) with \(L\) finite-dimensional over \(K\), then over \(\chi:\operatorname{Aut}_K L\to\mathbb C\) with a finite-dimensional complex representation of that group having character \(\chi\). The local-field, topological, valuation-extension and `IsGalois` classes are explicitly omitted at this endpoint. An elementary subgroup is the product of a cyclic subgroup of order prime to some prime \(p\) and a commuting \(p\)-group. These factors intersect trivially by the order condition. `spanZ` is the \(\mathbb Z\)-submodule generated by the induced functions from one-dimensional characters of elementary subgroups, so membership means a finite sum with integer coefficients. This gives a decomposition even for higher-dimensional irreducible characters. The proof uses the generic `mem_spanZ_of_mono` and supplies its global monomiality premise via `IsElementary.exists_eq_indLin`. The intermediate assembly alone is conditional on that premise. [Endpoint and omitted hypotheses](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/GroundTheory/LocalFields/ArtinConductorBrauer.lean#L176-L183) · [Assembly](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/GroundTheory/LocalFields/BrauerInductionAssembly.lean#L21) · [Monomiality](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/GroundTheory/LocalFields/BrauerInduction.lean#L14)

**Comparison.** Brauer’s theorem concerns arbitrary finite groups and is usually stated for virtual characters. The selected endpoint is the ordinary-character formulation for field automorphism groups, sufficient for integer linear combinations in that group’s virtual character ring. Its generic proof path is a better candidate for a standalone library. Mathlib representation APIs and the Lean evaluation on cyclotomic character values cover related problems. MathComp has class functions and induction machinery. The focused comparisons do not establish availability or priority for the full generic induction theorem. [Brauer, Annals 1947](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i307231) · [Boltje, canonical Brauer induction](https://smf.emath.fr/system/files/filepdf/AST_1990__181-182__31_0.pdf) · [Lean evaluation](https://lean-lang.org/eval/problems/brauer_character_in_cyclotomic/) · [MathComp class-function development](https://math-comp.github.io/htmldoc/mathcomp.character.classfun.html)

## 4. Krasner stabilizer inclusion

**Declaration.** `GlobalTheory.krasner_stabiliser` quantifies over a number field \(E\), an \(E\)-algebra field \(\Omega\), a valuation subring \(A\subseteq\Omega\), and \(\beta,\beta'\in\Omega\). The decomposition subgroup \(D\) is the group of \(E\)-automorphisms preserving \(A\). Assume the multiplicative valuation \(v(\beta'-\beta)<v(d\beta-\beta)\) for every \(d\in D\) moving \(\beta\). Then every \(\sigma\in D\) fixing \(\beta'\) fixes \(\beta\). The comparison is strict. If the orbit of \(\beta\) is trivial, the conclusion already holds. Otherwise the result compares actual distinct conjugates. [Statement and proof](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/KrasnerLemmaDecompositionGroupStabilizerVersion.lean#L18) · [Decomposition group](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/ReciprocityTheory/GaloisValuations/Global.lean#L15)

**Independent mathematical check.** If \(\sigma\) fixed \(\beta'\) and moved \(\beta\), the hypothesis for \(\sigma\) would give a bound on \(v(\beta-\beta')\). The hypothesis for \(\sigma^{-1}\), transported by the order-preserving valuation comparison under \(\sigma\), supplies the same strict bound on \(v(\beta'-\sigma\beta)\). Their sum is \(\beta-\sigma\beta\), contrary to the ultrametric inequality. This only requires preservation of the valuation *comparison*, so it works for the ordered value group used here. The familiar Krasner conclusion in Mathlib is generated-field membership under its ultrametric normed-field, algebraicity and completeness hypotheses. Recovering that conclusion from the subgroup argument needs an appropriate fixed-field statement. The overall fidelity comparison is unresolved. [Krasner reference](https://janschuetz.perso.math.cnrs.fr/lehre/ANT.pdf) · [Mathlib Krasner](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/Analysis/Normed/Field/Krasner.lean)

## 5. Cyclic linear-programming duality

**Declaration.** `Formalism.IncomingBounds.lemmaFa : LemmaFa` quantifies over an integer base \(\ell\ge3\), an even period \(d\ge2\) with its nonzero instance, and all nonnegative real budgets \(D_s\) on \(s\in\mathbb Z/d\). For each odd \(0<i<d\), a unit of nonnegative \(x_{s,i}\) costs \(\ell^i\) units from \(s\) and one from \(s+i\). The maximum throughput \(M\) is the supremum of \(\sum x_{s,i}\) under those vertex budgets. Set \(\gamma=\ell/(\ell^2-1)\) and \(F=\gamma\sum_s D_s-M\). The conclusion gives \(0\le\sigma_s\le1\) with \(\ell^i\sigma_s+\sigma_{s+i}\ge1\) for **all positive odd offsets**, equality \(F=\sum_sD_s(\gamma-\sigma_s)\), and greatestness of \(F\) among the objectives for feasible finite-offset duals. [Statement and LP definitions](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/IncomingBounds/IncomingBound.lean#L9-L61) · [Substantive proof module](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/IncomingBounds/FlowDegreeBounds/MaxFlowValueAttained.lean#L430)

**Independent checks.** Finite LP duality applies to the nonempty bounded primal. Clipping a dual coordinate to one preserves each covering inequality because any positive coefficient is at least one. It cannot increase the dual cost since \(D\ge0\). Reducing a positive odd offset modulo the even period gives a positive odd offset less than \(d\), and the coefficient \(\ell^i\) can only increase from that representative. This explains the extra all-offset clause. Go replays the two-letter primal \(\ell x+y\le a,\ x+\ell y\le b\), with nonnegative \(x,y\), and the two associated covering inequalities. It checks feasibility and equality of primal throughput and dual cost, which proves optimality for the 405 inputs. For example with \((\ell,a,b)=(5,3,2)\), the positive primal coordinates are \(13/24,7/24\) and the dual coordinates are \(1/6,1/6\); both objectives are \(5/6\). The certificate set covers zero budgets, unbalanced budgets, and the boundary between regimes. This is a reusable application of known duality. Isabelle’s AFP formalizes general LP duality, and the specific clipping and extension can be treated separately. [Exact checker](../byproducts/duality.go) · [Schrijver, Chapter 47](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~jean/math-deep.pdf) · [Isabelle AFP](https://www.isa-afp.org/entries/LP_Duality.html)
