# A3 · Independent audit of the coefficient estimate

## Human English

The submitted argument needs a lower bound on the divisibility of coefficients after differentiation. Individual coefficients of the input series can have a prime in the denominator. We traced how the proof handles them. Splitting variables into two copies reveals almost every coefficient without losing any divisibility. At a specific pair of prime powers, splitting can cost one power of the prime. The next estimate explicitly charges for that exception.

We independently proved the input coefficient bound and its factorial-weighted consequence under their original hypotheses. We also constructed a nonzero admissible local series. At the smallest allowed prime it has a raw coefficient of −1/5 whose differentiated coefficient has order 6. In a larger example the claimed lower bound is positive and the exact order exceeds it.

**Success B for these coefficient theorems.** We found no mathematical error in them. Whether all rows of the actual construction can pay the exceptional costs while retaining the advertised gain remains open in this audit. The independent verification status we inspected still listed the submission replay as running. These results do not settle RH or Dirichlet GRH.

## Statement and proof of the first coefficient bound

All notation and original hypotheses are in the [target and dependency trace](a3-coefficient-bound-target.md). We use the source's normalization v(p)=1. For an integer u prime to p, v(u)=0. Indeed integer norms are ≤1 by the ultrametric inequality, and Bézout applied to u and p rules out ‖u‖<1. This also gives the usual p-adic order for every rational integer in K. Write q=p², ρ=1/(q−1), h=1/(p−1), γ=h−ρ. Each variable has basic weight ρ. We assign zero coefficient weight +∞.

**1. Undo the letter log.** Write Lⱼ=Xⱼ+Σ_{e≥1} X_{shift(j,e)}^(qᵉ)/pᵉ, where the shift preserves the copy and subtracts 2e from the letter. The identity linear part gives a unique two-sided formal inverse g, by recursion in total degree. Every term in gⱼ has weight ≥ρ. To see this, use gⱼ=Xⱼ−Σ_{e≥1} g_{shift(j,e)}^(qᵉ)/pᵉ. A coefficient of degree N on the right uses only degrees <N in the nonlinear terms. The induction gives weight ≥qᵉρ−e≥ρ, since 1+q+⋯+q^(e−1)≥e. Products add weight lower bounds and finite coefficient sums preserve their minimum. No convergence in K is required.

**2. The two differences have full weight.** Fix either split and put D=Δb, C=exp(D). Its constant term is 1. By BHyp, C(L) is integral. Substitute g. Every term of C−1 now has weight ≥ρ>0 because a nonconstant monomial of C(L) uses at least one g. Moreover C has coefficients in K₀ and is torus invariant: the two copies of a letter transform by the same scalar, so splitting commutes with the action, as does formal exponentiation. Invariance makes Σₛ (−1)^s pˢ nₛ divisible by pᵈ−1 on each supported monomial (sum over both copies). Since d is even and (−1)^s pˢ≡1 mod p+1, its total degree is divisible by p+1. Thus its weighted order belongs to hℤ: its coefficient has integer order and ρ(p+1)=h. Positivity improves to weight ≥h. Take the formal logarithm of C. The j-th term of log(1+(C−1)) has weight ≥jh−vₚ(j)≥h, using p^vₚ(j)≤j and p^t≥1+(p−1)t. Since D has zero constant and the characteristic is zero, log C=D. This proves the strong bound for each coefficient of **both** split differences. [Actual split integrality hypotheses](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/Statement.lean#L85)

**3. Recover each coefficient of b.** For a supported index n choose a nontrivial split of its even-side multi-index u into u′+u″ with both nonzero. The coefficient of this split monomial in D is bₙ ∏ binom(uᵢ,u′ᵢ). The two subtracted terms have no mixed copies, and merging the copies determines n uniquely. The odd-side split works independently. If a side occupies two coordinates, send whole coordinates to opposite copies for factor 1. If a side has a single exponent m with base-p digit sum ≥2, it has a nontrivial split with p-unit factor. In the identity (1+T)^m ≡ ∏ⱼ(1+T^(pʲ))^(mⱼ) mod p, take one digit unit T^(pʲ) leaving a positive remainder. For m=pᵃ, a≥1, every interior binomial is divisible by p. The split at p^(a−1) loses exactly one: binom(pᵃ,p^(a−1))=p·binom(pᵃ−1,p^(a−1)−1) with the second factor a unit by the same digit identity. A singleton of exponent 1 has no split.

Hence a unit split gives weight ≥h for bₙ. The only remaining supported case has one p-power on each parity. B3 excludes both exponents being 1. At least one split then loses exactly one, giving weight ≥h−1. Invariance also forces p^(s+α)≡p^(t+β) mod pᵈ−1. The order of p modulo pᵈ−1 is d, since 0<pʲ−1<pᵈ−1 for 0<j<d. This is exactly the exceptional index predicate in LemmaS. Zeros satisfy both lower bounds. The proof applies to all fields, primes, even lengths, roots and series in the declaration. [Original proposition](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/Statement.lean#L98)

## From a letter coefficient to the quantitative jet

**4. Follow an exceptional coefficient through the linear substitution.** Let B, V, σ, κ be precisely as in LemmaRA. Linear forms H have integral coefficients and preserve total degree and letter labels. Every contribution to B therefore has basic weight ≥h−1. A contribution of weight <h can originate only at a bi-power in Step 3. In expanding a p^α-th power of a linear form, every mixed multinomial coefficient is divisible by p, by the Frobenius identity modulo p. Such a mixed term recovers the lost unit of valuation. Consequently every coefficient of B below weight h has index p^αeᵢ+p^βeⱼ. This argument permits cancellation when summing over entries. The exceptional-radius hypothesis refers to the surviving coefficient. For μ≤κ the extra weight Σ μᵢσᵢ is nonnegative and is ≥1 for a surviving bad index. In the new weights rᵢ=ρ+σᵢ every coefficient of B in the entire box [0,κ] thus has weight ≥h.

**5. Exponential, input series and differentiation.** B has zero constant. Each term Bʲ/j! of exp(±B) in this box has weight ≥jh−vₚ(j!)≥0. Multiplication never needs an index outside the box. The integral U composed with g and the H has weight ≥0 by Step 1, valid with any entry set and finite label set. Therefore the unweighted coefficient of V exp(±B) at κ has valuation ≥−Σᵢκᵢ(ρ+σᵢ). On differentiation, the valuation of ∏κᵢ! is Σ(κᵢ−sₚ(κᵢ))/(p−1), by counting multiples of pʲ in each factorial. For κᵢ≤A and A≥1, sₚ(κᵢ)/(p−1)≤⌊logₚ A⌋+1, including κᵢ=0. Combining the two inequalities gives exactly

v((∏κᵢ!) [T^κ] V exp(±B)) ≥ Σκᵢ(γ−σᵢ) − |I|(logₚ A+1).

This reconstructs `lemma_R_log_loss` without assuming a coefficient valuation conclusion. It explains the **1** in the dimension-times-logarithm loss. On the frame entry set |I|=d N₀ N₁. The proof requires the stated radius inequalities only at bad indices ≤κ. It gives no assertion that every desired σ pays for those indices. [Source statement](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/StatementG.lean#L12) · [Use of the radius condition for a frame coefficient](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/TheoremAOneMonomialLogLoss.lean#L188)

## Independent check of the 11

Let S=Σw, T_w=Σ_T w, W=(k−1)w and L=2·1_T w, so D+L=W+E, D≥0. In the finite LP of the target report the dual prices y≥0 obey pⁱyₛ+y_(s+i)≥1. Prices above 1 can be capped at 1. For nonnegative capacities the feasible primal contains zero, has bounded objective, and ordinary finite LP duality gives optimal primal and dual solutions. Thus F is the maximum of ΣDₛ(γ−yₛ) over these capped duals.

At capacities W, choose an optimal dual y and flow x. Wherever yₛ≥2/p the budget is saturated, and every outgoing edge is slack on the dual side (pⁱyₛ≥2). Complementary slackness forces zero outgoing flow there. Those Wₛ are entirely incoming, so their total is ≤Σx≤ΣW/(p+1), by summing all capacity constraints. Since k−1>0, the corresponding sum of wₛ is ≤S/p. At other letters (yₛ−γ)₊≤1/p since γ≥1/p. Everywhere it is ≤1. Hence ΣEₛ(yₛ−γ)₊≤8S/p. Use the same y as a feasible dual at W+E to get F(W+E)≥F(W)−8S/p. Conversely every dual price is nonnegative, hence F(D+L)≤F(D)+2γT_w. Since 2γ≤3/p for p≥3, these give both inequalities of proposition_six and F(D)≥F(W)−11S/p. There is no rank, conductor or orbit-length factor hidden in 11. Repeated use still subtracts the corresponding sum of losses. Since topTerm≥baseTerm term by term, the comparison gives Cor2 if ThmA is available. ## Constructing a genuinely admissible mixed series

Set p=5, d=2, q=25. The shift by 2 is the identity. Work over ℂ₅ with rational coefficients and a primitive 24th root. Write L(X)=Σ X^(qᵉ)/5ᵉ and g=L⁻¹. Use b(X,Y)=log(1+g(X)^5 g(Y)). This choice tests nonzero mixed coefficients with denominators. All examples below instantiate the uniform **local** theorem. Their occurrence in a particular global lift has not been established.

* **B1–B3.** Rational coefficients belong to the required field. L and g commute with scaling by a 24th root since q≡1 mod 24. The torus sends (X,Y) to (uX,u^(−5)Y), so b is invariant. Its supported indices have X-degree ≥5 and Y-degree ≥1.
* **B4 for every degree.** F(X,Y)=g(L(X)+L(Y)) has 5-integral coefficients. To verify this, put A=F(X^q,Y^q), C=F(X,Y)^q. From L(F)=L(X)+L(Y) and L(Z)=Z+L(Z^q)/5 one obtains F=X+Y+Σ_{j≥0}(A^(qʲ)−C^(qʲ))/5^(j+1). At total degree n the sum uses only coefficients of F of smaller degree. Replace F by its lower truncation. By induction it is integral; Frobenius on its coefficients modulo 5 gives A≡C mod 5. Binomial expansion shows that raising congruent integral series to the fifth power raises the modulus by at least one power of 5. Raising to qʲ thus makes the numerator divisible by 5^(j+1). This proves the induction, starting with the identity linear terms. Now exp(Δ_even b)(L) equals (1+F(X,X′)^5 Y)/((1+X^5 Y)(1+X′^5 Y)). For the odd split it equals (1+X^5 F(Y,Y′))/((1+X^5Y)(1+X^5Y′)). Both have integral coefficients and constant 1. Their coefficients lie in the unramified field, as required.
* **B0 and the operator.** Choose ep=sign=1. For each integral parameter polynomial U set Θ=U(g(Z))∏_{a,b′}(1+g(Z_(0,a,b′))^5 g(Z_(1,a,b′))). Substitution of L gives precisely B0. For a single row at each letter use the first output column for every box. Take the X-index with all factors on input row 0 at both letters. Only entry (0,0) in each block contributes. For U=XʳYˢ in this entry pair, the required coefficient equals n!m! [XⁿYᵐ]g(X)ʳg(Y)ˢ(1+g(X)^5g(Y)). There is no assumption that a raw coefficient is integral.
* **Ranks and ordinary-size limits.** With T empty take rows (n),(m), E=0, w=(n,m)/(k−1). All table entries have positive weights and satisfy the Cor2 identities. p=5 and d=2 are minimal for JetLoc. At a=5, HidaSetting forces N₀≥5 and N₁>N₀, hence 5,6 are minimal ranks. With k=3 and positive one-row degrees, (1,1) is the smallest pair off T. The first possible mixed letter monomial in this two-letter torus has total degree 6, attained at (5,1). The legal origin for the isolated LemmaS alone also allows p=3. The one-letter, ramified and above-cutoff estimates are outside this experiment.

The zero-degree boundary is legal for Cor2 as well. Use two empty diagrams, both letters in T, k=3, w≡1, E=0, U=1, a=5 and ranks 5,6. The empty minor product gives the constant 1, and every nonconstant output coefficient is zero. Both top terms and caps vanish. ThmA gives −120 and Cor2 gives 1/6−22/5−120. This local example fails the additional global top-row condition 2w≤first row.

**A minor with cancellation.** At a=5 with ranks 5,6 take ν₀=(1,1) as a column of height 2, ν₁=(1), k=3, T empty, w=(2,1)/2. Choose output columns 0,1 at the first letter and 0 at the second. **All coefficients in the positive example.** The fifth row also admits a short check for *every* choice of output columns and index. This exceeds both 42061/4 and 137641/340. The bound holds for the entire output family in this one instance, independently of any numerical cancellation.

## Quantitative relevance, verification and next action

The local loss can exceed the entire gain, as the first four exact cases show. The fifth has a strictly positive bound. Its raw coefficient has order −1, so applying the same bound before differentiation would fail on this legal instance. A3 proves constants for the coefficient and LP estimates uniformly in their declared parameters. It supplies no lower bound on the global sum of FOrb−errF along actual families. In particular, for a single served second orbit even a positive FOrb pays the five explicit terms in the [target](a3-coefficient-bound-target.md). For positive **net** gain at that place FOrb must exceed their sum. The global argument may use gains from other places; a loss at one place proves no gap. Below the cutoff the submitted proof inserts FOrb−errF into one table, above it uses zero, then invokes a separate lower estimate for the sum over complementary places. The conditional endpoint 1/2+9ε beats 2/3 for 0<ε<1/54. Verifying its hypotheses for arbitrarily small ε still requires uniform control of these summed costs and the remaining A1/A2 family links. [Actual table and summed estimate](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/LocalSystemsLowerBoundsSecondRealPlace.lean#L53) · [Endpoint hypotheses](a2-simultaneous-admissibility-audit.md)

At a served place the exact coverage required by the caller is

$$
\operatorname{lowS}(u,w)\ \le\
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{t\in\mathrm{reps}(X,p)}
  \mathbf 1_{p\le X''}\bigl(F_{\mathrm{Orb}}(j,p,(1,t))-\mathrm{errF}(j,p,(1,t))\bigr)
 - \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} dG(j).
$$

Here the indicator uses the family cutoff at D.R.n and (1,t) denotes the second embedding. The source requires L.FDef, dG(0)=0 and dG(j)≤vₚ(DGam) for every j. This is where repeating the stages also repeats the logarithmic losses. The coefficient theorem bounds the jets that enter this sum. Its hypotheses alone specify neither lowS nor dG.
