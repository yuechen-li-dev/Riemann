# A1 audit of the claimed Dirichlet GRH proof

**Outcome B for one lemma.** We independently proved `Formalism.DivisionValues.divisor_into_dual_shift` with its original quantifiers. We found no counterexample to it. The lemma spends an already established local divisor. Obtaining a large enough divisor at the places and stages used by the analytic estimate is a separate claim. A1 leaves RH and Dirichlet GRH open in Riemann. [Exact target, independent proof and dependency trace](a1-claude-grh-target.md)

## What the proof actually uses

At each finite place w, the local pairing at the selected character tuple μ(w) must have valuation at least σ(w) on **every** admissible pair of lattice vectors whose pairing is nonzero. Given the unit twist, pairing identity, integer rounding bound and two localization identities, dividing the first lattice by the prescribed local factors leaves its global pairing integral. The proof works by checking membership in each local ring of integers and intersecting those rings. The caller uses the resulting dual-lattice containment in its global frame estimate. We followed the proof bodies through the arithmetic window, half-plane nonvanishing, extension to imprimitive characters and the zeta specialization. [Original proposition](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/QuotientMetricDivisors/DivIntoDualShift.lean#L133) · [Selected tuple and application](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteForms/PairingConditionOfInverseMatrixIdentity.lean#L393) · [Use of the specific clause](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/TopDensityNGeSixteen.lean#L716) · [Endpoints](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Main.lean)

The invariant is the local ideal generated by the pairing values. Over ℤ its generator is the gcd. Taking a character average, summing the pairings, or multiplying them computes different quantities. The source supplies a pointwise period bound through `val2AllAtRF`. A1 found no use of an inference from divisibility of an average to divisibility of every coefficient on this path. The simpler question has a sharp answer even over ℚ. Take B(x,y)=xy, all lattices ℤ, identity row maps, unit twists and σ=τ=0 everywhere. All the gluing hypotheses hold and B(1,1)=1. Consequently the original hypotheses alone cannot force even one extra prime factor. [Period-to-pairing transport](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GroupLattices/ServJointF.lean#L84)

## Exact tests and the counterexample boundary

The Go kernel specializes the original lemma to K=ℚ, global row lattice R=ℤ, local modules M(w)=ℤ, initial column lattice ℤᵈ and B(x,y)=(Σ cᵢxᵢ)y. At the tested prime p the local row map and twist are integer multipliers, both 1 by default. Their valuations must each be zero. The second row matrix is the identity and the local P is defined by the required pairing identity. Shifting multiplies the column lattice by $p^{-\tau}$. At other primes this is a unit, the local maps are identities and the bounds and shifts are zero. Each subsequent step starts from the preceding shifted basis. Checking every basis pairing is necessary and sufficient here by bilinearity over the local ring. An empty basis represents dimension zero.

An exhaustive check of 1,156 signed two-entry rows and prime choices verifies the equality between gcd order and minimum order on that finite box, including the all-zero row. Separate tests cover negative shifts, the integer $3^{300}$, negative rational order and exact cancellation. The artifact runner rejects composite primes and malformed inputs. All certificate arithmetic uses integers and rationals. The shift exponent is restricted to |τ|≤4096 in this runner.

The target allows any number field and places no restrictions on conductor, primitivity or lift count. These issues enter through other declarations. Assigning a small conductor or character label to (1,3) would give no evidence about those claims.

## Can the saving reach the critical line?

**Conditionally yes at the global estimate. The size of the underlying arithmetic saving remains undetermined in A1.** The announcement describes a preliminary obstruction at 2/3 and a target of 1/2. The gluing lemma makes no assertion of a positive gain. Even the finite-sum comparison in the upstream proof allows equality at every stage. At exceptional primes the source explicitly switches off its served gain. A positive conclusion after composition needs bounds at the remaining places, with the same chosen data, and control of the accumulated losses. [Announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Gain definition](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a172c464fd4bcfc4f53e394dcba45d742b1409a6/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/WeilJacquetShalikaLaw.lean#L37)

In particular, `LinkDIV` requires constants C and x₀ that work for all x≥x₀, every family index and every word. One clause requires

$$
 \mu_{\rm dual}+h(u)-F_o \le
 \mathrm{MAIN}(u)+C\,|TT|\,a\,L_a+
 C\,m(B_0+N_c)x^{C_{\rm abs}\varepsilon}.
$$

The top-level caller supplies Cabs=2. The comparison also uses the other division clauses, rank, design existence, global estimates and analytic host supply for every primitive character. The parameter λ=3/2 in the final comparison is a scale. Reading it as an inverse zero-free exponent would supply no estimate of the saving. A1 verified the arithmetic identity for composing the local gain inequalities in the target report. Positivity and size relative to this global error remain unverified here. The exact downstream threshold is visible. The tied-window data require m ≥ x^(1/2−ε). Given a compatible family and the Chebyshev comparison, the proof obtains Λ.Gm(x) ≤ x^(1/2+(eD+eL+7)ε) for all sufficiently large x in the selected windows. On this route `topDesWin` supplies eD=2 and eL=0, so the exponent is 1/2+9ε. For 0<ε<min(ε₁,1/54) it lies strictly below 2/3. For any prescribed real part β>1/2 there is likewise an ε with 1/2+9ε<β. The pinned proof uses the window coverage and the analytic implication `FormBAt` to pass from growth on those windows to nonvanishing. This computation identifies what would suffice and exactly where uniformity in ε enters. It supplies no independent proof that the required families or growth bounds exist. ## Compiler evidence and reproduction

M13's symbolic expressions and M18/M23's rational intervals serve Fourier and analytic contracts. This problem uses exact valuations of rational lattice pairings. A1 follows M24/M25's division of labor with an Oct search, a persisted input, independent exact Go replay and a separate opt-in source-trace check. The existing graph carries evidence, assumptions, parent IDs, source links and trust flags. A small external proposition type identifies the pinned predicate and optional finite instance. The A1 rules check the supplied premise signatures. Ordinary proof attempts now also check theorem trust along their path, as target certification already did.

The graph records six source implications. The selected local bound, the dual inclusion, the cost, the eventual tied window, the Chebyshev growth estimate, the zero-free region and RH all remain open applications. The two finite computations carry `CertifiedComputationEvidence` only after Go replay checks their specified inputs. Standalone graph certification has no blanket trust rule for external computation records. Reproduce the finite conclusions with the replay command.
