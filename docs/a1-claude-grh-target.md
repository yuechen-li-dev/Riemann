# A1 · Which divisor enters the proof?

**Target.** `Formalism.DivisionValues.divisor_into_dual_shift` converts lower bounds on the values of bilinear pairings, one bound for each finite place, into an integral global pairing after division. Its conclusion is valid under its stated hypotheses. The size of the divisor is an **input**. This distinction locates a quantitative obligation that can be attacked upstream.

* Quantify over K of type `Type` with `Field`, `NumberField`, `StarRing`, and d,D ∈ ℕ. Let R be any O-submodule of Kᴰ and B any K-bilinear pairing Kᵈ × Kᴰ → K.

* Quantify over O-submodules N,N′ of Kᵈ, σ from finite places to ℝ and τ from finite places to ℤ. For **every** w, φ ∈ N and F ∈ M(w), require P(w)(φ,F) = 0 or σ(w) ≤ v_w(P(w)(φ,F)). Require τ(w) ≤ ⌈σ(w)⌉ for every w.
* Require `IsShift N N' τ`. For every w there exists a nonzero a_w ∈ K with v_w(a_w) = −τ(w) and N′_w = (a_w N)_w. Require also R_w = (ι(w)π(w)M(w))_w. The two matrix images here are the source's nested `matLat` operations.

**Arithmetic operation.** The valuation lower bound applies to the image of a bilinear pairing of two modules. Where matrices of generators exist, its content is the ideal generated by **all** generator pairings. At a prime this takes their minimum nonzero valuation, with the zero ideal having infinite depth. Over ℤ a generator of this ideal is the gcd. Its RH-path caller chooses a possibly different tuple μ(w) at each place and uses a common row lattice identified locally with its image. It neither sums character coefficients nor multiplies them. Calling this a gcd across every Hecke character would specify the wrong indexing set. The order of the choices matters. At different primes the proof can use different integral realizations of the period, provided their row lattices glue and the transported pairing is the same global B up to local units. This can combine prime-by-prime divisors in one global lattice. A character sum occurring in the construction of a period would still need a bound on every required lattice pairing.

## Independent proof for all the stated variables

Fix w and let A=O_w be its discrete valuation ring. For nonzero P-values the integer v_w(P) ≥ σ(w) implies v_w(P) ≥ ⌈σ(w)⌉ ≥ τ(w). Including zeros, this says P(w)(N,M(w)) ⊆ 𝔪_w^τ(w) inside K. Here negative exponents denote fractional ideals. By bilinearity the containment extends to the A-spans. Multiplying the first argument by a_w subtracts τ(w) from every nonzero valuation. Hence P(w)(N′_w,M(w)_w) ⊆ A. Division by the unit ζ(w) preserves A. The matrix identity and the equality defining R_w now give B(N′_w,R_w) ⊆ A. For fixed φ ∈ N′, x ∈ R the single element B(φ,x) therefore lies in every O_w. Their intersection in K is O because a nonzero element outside O has a negative exponent at a prime in the factorization of its principal fractional ideal. Zero is integral as well. This proves the original implication, including zero-dimensional and zero-module cases. Discreteness, bilinearity, localization and factorization in the ring of integers are the mathematical inputs. The Go replay checks the explicitly realized rational instances.
